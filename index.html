How the surfaces are built

Measured + pressure slope  Tt = 56 − 3m − 0.5m² + (P − 0.1)/7.8  (°C; m in mol/kg; P in MPa)

  • NaCl effect at 0.1 MPa: transitions of 56, 52, 48, 36 and 20 °C at 0, 1, 2, 4 and 6 mol/kg (Blount & Dickson 1973, tabulated by Berdugo et al. 2008). The quadratic passes through all five within 0.5 °C.
  • Pressure effect: +1 °C per 7.8 MPa, the slope Berdugo et al. adopt from Blount & Dickson. It was measured in pure water and is added unchanged at every salinity.

PHREEQC Pitzer

  • PHREEQC 3 (phreeqpython) with the pitzer.dat database. For each NaCl concentration and pressure, the brine is equilibrated with anhydrite and the temperature where gypsum's saturation index reaches zero is found by bisection.
  • Computed on a 26 × 11 grid (0–6.2 mol/kg; 0.1–100 MPa) and interpolated between nodes. Salt and pressure are treated together through Pitzer activity coefficients and molar volumes.

How they compare. Both give 56 °C in pure water and about +12 to +13 °C per 100 MPa. They diverge with salinity: at 6 mol/kg PHREEQC gives about 34 °C, against about 20 °C measured, and Zen (1965) and Hardie (1967) also place the transition below 20 °C near halite saturation. The measured model is likely closer at high salinity; the PHREEQC gap probably reflects Ca–SO₄ parameters fitted mainly to seawater-type brines. This attribution is an interpretation, not tested here.

  • Pressure here acts on both the fluid and the solids. If the rock carries a higher load than the pore fluid, the behavior differs (MacDonald 1953).
  • Both are equilibrium models. Below about 100 °C anhydrite reacts very slowly, so either phase can persist metastably near the boundary.
  • Halite saturation is taken as 6.15 mol/kg (25 °C); values above 6 mol/kg are a short extrapolation.

Sources: Blount & Dickson (1973) · Berdugo et al. (2008) · Hardie (1967) · MacDonald (1953) · Monnin (1990) · PHREEQC (USGS)
